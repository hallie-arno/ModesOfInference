# P Values Are Not Evidence: Navigating The Gap Across Modes Of Inference

-   Authors: Hallie E. Arno, Robert J. Blackmore, Samantha E. Crowley, S. M. R. Evon, Ian A. Fleming, C. Tyler Fountain, Stephan Hamisch, Emily M. Monk, Alec L. Robitaille, David C. Schneider

This repository contains the code accompanying the paper [“P Values Are Not Evidence: Navigating The Gap Across Modes Of Inference”](10.1002/ece3.74419)


## Abstract

The use of p-values and statistical significance testing as the primary mode of inference
in science has been long debated among statisticians and more recently among ecologists.
Ecological literature has discussed embracing other modes of inference, such as Bayesian
statistics and the use of direct likelihood inference. We reviewed ecology, evolution, and
environmental science literature covering the past three decades to investigate trends in the use
of different modes of inference over time and to assess the degree of discrepancy between
reported p-values and calculated likelihood ratios. p-Values and likelihood ratios were found to
scale in a more direct way for single degree of freedom tests, but in a more complex manner for
models with more than one parameter. These results demonstrate the degree to which the
likelihood ratio (a measure of evidence) differs from a p-value, a measure of uncertainty that is
widely presented as a measure of evidence among publications within the fields of ecology and
evolution. Additionally, our investigation revealed that modes of inference within ecological
studies changed little over the 20-year time period, indicating a failure to adopt alternative modes
of inference. We also found downward trends in the reporting of p-value thresholds (e.g., “p <
0.05”) in two journals, with no change in three others. The prevalence of the divergence in the
measure of evidence and the p-value measure of uncertainty remains to be confirmed in other
journals, time points, and subfields. An overreliance on p-values alone can alter the conclusions
drawn from research, impacting management of species and ecosystems. We recommend that
authors report both a measure of evidence and uncertainty, and justify their mode of inference in
an attempt to produce statistical results that are comprehensive, replicable, and reliably
interpretable.
